Notion, the Loom,
and the record

Three planes and four flows. A Notion workspace where people author and decide, three service identities that carry things across, and a GitHub repository that is the only source of truth — with a protected CI plane underneath that reads files and signed envelopes, and never a vendor API.

AI proposes; humans and a protected control plane dispose. Notion is where work is authored. Git is where it counts. Nothing in this drawing changes which one is which.

The four flows, in one line each

Direction is the whole design. Three of the four are one-way, and the fourth — the one that carries authority — deliberately does not pass through the service that carries it.

FlowDirectionCarried byWhat makes it safe
1 · Projectionrecord → floorsvc-floor-projector The token cannot write git — proved by a capability-matrix probe, recorded. A projection that lies is a stale board, never a false approval.
2 · Freezefloor → recordsvc-floor-freezer Explicitly human-triggered; a freeze is an intent, like a merge. The exporter fails closed on unknown blocks, truncation or permission gaps, and stamps the export digest.
3 · Decisionfloor → recordsvc-floor-bridge The webhook is a signal, not a record: signature verified, deduplicated on event id, current state refetched. Bot and agent authors are rejected outright.
4 · Assertionhuman → IdP → recordthe bridge, as courier only The gate verifies the assertion's issuer, never the bridge's. A service key signing for a human authenticates the worker, not the approver — and the contract refuses that shape.
Why flow 4 is drawn over the top

If the proof of a human decision travelled through the bridge, a compromised bridge could manufacture any registered human's approval for any change. That was finding F1. The correction is architectural, not procedural: the assertion is minted on TB-0, where the bridge has no reach, and it binds a nonce equal to the digest of this exact decision — so it cannot be replayed onto another one.

Where this actually is

The drawing shows the target architecture. This is how much of it exists today, so the picture is not mistaken for a deployment.

Shipped · loom 2.0.0-rc.13

The approval-attestation contract and its two-signature refusal · the PA-gate extension (mandatory-when-compiled) · both issuer registries, kept deliberately separate · the identity registry including the three floor service identities, none holding an approval role · nine generated floor template pairs with a parity gate · the ADR and SDR templates.

Declared, not active

No shipped profile compiles the approval_attestation capability, so nothing demands envelope-backed approvals today. oidc-step-up and sigstore report UNVERIFIED-HERE until an adopter pins provider metadata and records activation evidence. All four evidence adapters ship declared until their own real evidence lands.

Specified, not built

Every Notion-side surface: the workspace, the projector, the freezer, the bridge. They need a workspace and a token, and the residency review (P1) must be signed by data-protection and risk-second-line first. The P6 identity map is fully specified and no gate reads it yet.

Production-blocked, by design

Decision routing (WS5). ADR-0005 settled the mechanism on 25 Jul 2026 — step-up OIDC with the nonce-binding condition — but the entry gate has a second condition that stands: an independent second-line review of the workstream's design. Until then every decision surface runs labelled non-authoritative.

The residual worth naming

The identity-provider subject is bound but not joined: the assertion's subject and the registry identity sit side by side under one signature, so neither can be altered afterwards — but nothing independent yet asserts they are the same person. That join is the P6 identity map, which no gate reads. Until it does, a compromised bridge holding a valid assertion for subject A could name registry identity B, and every check in the drawing would still pass.
