The Loom · AI-DLC

How a regulated institution builds software with AI.

Not a tool. Not a framework to replace the SDLC. A way of working in which agents do the building, controls are always on, and leaders take the decisions that matter — with evidence for every one of them.

The Loom is the deliverable. UAE Open Finance was the first solution built on it.

The problem

AI ships in minutes. The governance around it was built for humans typing.

Volume

Change outruns the review

DevOps drove a hundredfold increase in change. Agents add another. You cannot put a human in front of every pull request, so either review becomes theatre or delivery stalls.

Evidence

The audit trail starts too late

Pipelines record the commit onward. Intent, requirement, risk decision and design rationale — the things an auditor actually asks about — live in chats and heads.

Accountability

Nobody knows who built it

When an agent writes, reviews and fixes code, "who is accountable" has no answer unless the way of working produces one.

Trust

Agents can narrate evidence

A model can write a convincing test report without running a test. Second line will not accept agentic delivery until that is impossible by construction.

The Loom in one picture

One diagram. Highlight your role.

Five parts. The pattern is what the institution already knows. The warp is every control, on for every pass. The harnesses are the two halves of the work. The shuttle is the agents. The cloth is what ships — with its evidence woven in, not attached afterwards.

The two harnesses

Discovery and delivery, run as a double diamond with gates a leader owns.

Step through one pass of the shuttle with a running example at Meridian Trust, a fictional UAE bank. Two scenarios: an operational fix, and a business problem where the answer turns out to be Open Finance. Ember marks where a human decides; everything else is deterministic or agentic.

The warp

Controls are threads, not tickets.

A control that has to be remembered will be forgotten at agent speed. In the Loom every control is structural: it holds for every pass whether or not anyone thinks about it.

  • Deterministic hard gates Unit, integration, contract, security, schema diff, SBOM. Machine-evaluable, no opinion involved. A failed gate is a stop, not a comment.
  • Three hard stops on every loop Maximum iterations, no-progress detection, token and cost ceiling. An unbounded agent loop touching production or a certification path is a risk event, not a cost issue.
  • Actor attribution on every artefact Human, or which agent on which model and harness version, with which tool permissions — and which human accepted it.
  • No self-attestation Evidence is machine-derived from a real tool run. An agent never evidences its own work, and never narrates a result it did not observe.
  • Risk tagging at refinement Every backlog item is tagged against the institution's risk taxonomy before work starts, by a virtual head of risk, not after the fact by a tired human.
  • Evidence substrate Every gate, decision and attestation lands in an immutable record — Kosli in the reference implementation — so the audit package is a query, not a project.
  • Tiered human gates Deterministic tasks run unattended. Agentic review handles the judgement calls. Humans take the gates that carry risk or intent. Nothing else reaches them.

The pattern

Institutional context, packaged as skills the agents can read.

Skills are the compounding asset. Each one encodes something the institution knows — a regulation, a product, a house style, a risk taxonomy — so that every agent on every pass starts from the same understanding. They are the reason the tenth build is faster than the first.

Plus the compound step: every pass ends by asking what the system learned, and writing it back — a steering-file line, a new review rule, a skill update. Skip it and you have ordinary engineering with AI help.

Leaders in the loop

Decision-grade proofs, not status reports.

The Loom does not ask leaders to review code. It asks them to take four decisions per pass, each with the evidence attached, and gives them the same diagram engineering works from.

Gate 1

Which problem

Agents synthesise the context pack and surface the candidate problems. The leader picks one. Recorded, attributed, timestamped.

Gate 2

Approve the case, lock the spec

Spec, prototype and investment case lock together — what will be built, what it looks like, what it costs and returns. A go here is a go for unattended build within the warp.

Gate 3

Accept the proof

A working, tested, evidenced increment — not a deck about one. Accepting it is a decision with an audit trail.

Gate 4

Release

Risk classification and evidence drive the approval. The change advisory board reviews exceptions, not everything.

What it is, in a sentence

The Loom is the how that standards refuse to write.

A controls catalogue says what must hold true. An evidence platform records what happened. The Loom is the way of working in between: it produces the evidence the catalogue asks for, at agent speed, with a human accountable for every decision that matters.

It is offered as a practice to adopt, a reference implementation to run, and a set of skills to make your own.
