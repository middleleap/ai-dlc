MiddleLeap Partnership note

Kosli × MiddleLeap

Kosli proves what happened. The Loom governs how the work is done.

Two platforms, one lifecycle, and a seam in the middle that neither of them owns today. This note sets out where they meet, what the FINOS catalogue does not yet cover, and the seven capabilities that would close both gaps.

The two platforms

Kosli's chain of custody begins at the commit. The Loom's ends when code is handed to the pipeline. Between them sits a shared surface — attestations, policy definitions, and the context graph agents read before they act.

Where the catalogue stops

The FINOS SDLC controls framework — launched under the DevOps Automation SIG by Deutsche Bank, Morgan Stanley and Kosli — is building the shared vocabulary the industry has never had: a risk catalogue and a mitigation catalogue in RFC-style language, mapped outward to NIST SP 800-53, FFIEC and OWASP, with EU AI Act, ISO 42001 and NIST AI 600-1 reference sets already wired in. It defines what must hold true and deliberately refuses to say how.

It also reaches further upstream than most people assume. Two draft mitigations — a requirements repository (mi-4) and requirements approval for release (mi-20) — sit before the first commit. Both are the least complete entries in the catalogue: no regulatory references, no implementation pattern. The Loom's intent-and-spec harness is that pattern.

Which leaves two holes, and they are the same shape as the seam above.

Not in the risk catalogue

  • Non-determinism as a delivery risk in its own right
  • Attribution of agent-authored change
  • Prompt and context injection into the pipeline
  • Model and tool drift across a single release
  • Evidence integrity when the author is an agent

The AI reference frameworks are plumbed in, but none of the eleven risks is AI-native. AI governance lives in the separate FINOS AI Governance Framework, so no one currently owns the intersection of "AI writes the code" and "SDLC controls." That intersection is the Loom's entire subject.

Not in scope at all

Every outward mapping is US or EU. There is no CBUAE, no PDPL, and no Shariah-governance dimension — the jurisdiction where Kosli already has a flagship customer and no control vocabulary to sell against.

Proposed contribution: MiddleLeap drafts a set of AI-native risk definitions for the catalogue, co-authored with Kosli, plus a CBUAE and PDPL mapping for the existing mitigations. The risk vocabulary goes public; the harnesses that implement it stay proprietary.

State of the catalogue at its last readiness report, 20 June 2026: eleven risks, nineteen mitigations, one working-group approval. Identifiers cited in this note are drafts and will move. Alignment is claimed against draft controls, not conformance.

Lifecycle coverage

Switch between the two states. Kosli today covers half the lifecycle well and the other half not at all — and the uncovered half is where AI now generates the most work and the least verifiable evidence.
