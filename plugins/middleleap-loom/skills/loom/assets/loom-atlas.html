The Loom, drawn

An ambiguous idea goes in. Audit-ready software comes out, the same way every time.

The Loom is a reusable way for a regulated institution to build with AI. Set it up once, then weave any solution on it. This page draws the machine: what the institution prepares, how a problem travels the loop, where the people stand, and what a gate actually reads.

Every diagram below is a mechanism from the canon, not a metaphor. The one metaphor the method keeps is exact, and it is drawn first.

Before the first problem

The institution prepares its pattern

Why this is the moat

If a competitor copied the whole codebase tomorrow, this is what they would still lack. Context compounds cycle after cycle, and bad context compounds as readily as good. Curation is a control, which is why nothing here is generated from memory.

Where the people stand

The Factory Floor proposes. Git disposes.

Most of the people a regulated build depends on never open a repository and should not have to. The floor is their guided surface. Nothing a gate trusts lives there, and enforcement is identical with the floor present or absent.

Two roles, deliberately unequal

  • The author owns the content of the problem. A product manager, on the floor, in guided forms whose banner says what becomes of what they type.
  • The facilitator owns the record. One engineer with Claude Code and the plugin, at the machine.
  • Neither approves anything. Approval, where discovery has any, is a second human merging a PR.

What a gate actually reads

Every link is a file. Nothing is remembered.

The regulated context is one chain from obligation to evidence, and the institutional context binds into the plan by digest. A control that exists as prose nobody reads is not on this drawing.

Honest state

Nothing here reads as more controlled than it is.

The method grades itself on five states, from absent to organisationally enforced. This strip is the grade for what this page draws. Green is a gate that fails the build. Gold is defined and read by humans. Grey is the adopter's to wire.

Mechanically validatedD1–D9, Q1–Q5, the BrainKit gate

Pure-Node validators; a missing register, a stale digest or an omitted canonical section fails the build. Schema 1.0 packages keep passing.

Mechanically validatedThe waist and the floor's ten gates

No feature item enters delivery without a green hand-off that names it. Freeze stamps, drift, parity and floor-only crossings are checked in CI.

Defined, read by humansStrategic intents (SI-*)

A problem statement and a business case cite the intent they serve. No gate reads the citation yet; the decision authority does.

Defined, read by humansThe business case and its cost band

The develop skill checks the chosen direction lands inside the band. It is an instruction to the facilitator, not a script.

Adopter-side wiringThe floor itself

The bundle ships the exporter, fetcher, gates and forms. The workspace, the projector, the freezer and the hosting carry no vendor client or token by design.

BlockedDecision routing (WS5)

Catalog B ships declared, not active, until an independent second-line review of the design. The funding decision is recorded at the machine.

UnsignedMiddleLeap's own residency record

Drafted, not signed. A floor cannot exist on an unsigned record, and the gate that enforces that is live.

The standing riskComprehension debt

The review gate is the one resource that does not scale. The decision log makes the agent's reasoning replayable; whether the human at the gate is still reading is named, not measured.
