# Synthesis — cross-bank-money

> Discover (converge). Every theme traces to signals in `research-log.md` (gate D5). The
> prioritisation method is stated so the ranking can be argued with, not just read.

## Themes

| Theme id | Theme | Traces to signals | So-what (why it matters) |
|---|---|---|---|
| T-1 | Customers cannot see their whole money picture, and Meridian advises on one slice | S-001, S-002, S-004, S-009 | The sponsor's "matter in everyday decisions" fails on visibility first; the contact centre absorbs the cost |
| T-2 | The bank does manual, perishable work to bridge the gap | S-003, S-002 | Liability letters and repeat enquiries are cost today, and the output is stale within a month |
| T-3 | Knowing and acting are different asks with different risk | S-009, S-007, S-006 | Most demand is to know; acting (moving money) raises consent scope, payment-status and liability questions that change the risk class |
| T-4 | Meridian has half the capability and none of the ownership for the other half | S-005, S-008 | Live as a data provider, never a recipient; no owner for the receiving-consent journey; the platform will admit an AI-assisted team only behind executable constraints |

## Prioritisation

- **Method:** impact × reach ÷ effort, scored 1–5 by the consulted roles `[synthetic]`; effort is discovery-and-authorisation effort, not build effort (no build is sized here)
- **Result:** T-1 leads; T-3 is carried as a constraint on T-1's framing rather than a competing theme; T-4 is a precondition, not a problem

## Candidate problem (leading theme)

T-1 is the problem worth taking into Define: customers managing money across several banks
cannot see their whole position, and Meridian — seeing one slice — cannot help them with the
decision they are actually making. T-3 shapes the framing: the leading hypothesis is about
*knowing*, and *acting* (moving money between banks) is held as a separate, later hypothesis
that carries its own obligations. T-4 says what must be true before any of it can be built:
the receiving-consent journey needs an owner, and the AI-assisted team needs executable
constraints, not a policy document.
